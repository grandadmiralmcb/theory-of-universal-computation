Nine modules

    A course for people who don't do the math

    Counting the World

    There is a way of doing physics in which almost everything reduces to one question: how many strings would you have to cut?

    This is a nine-module tour of the expression-tree approach to physics. It has no equations you need to solve and no prior physics. What it does have is one picture — knots tied together with strings — that turns out to carry gravity, entropy, black holes and the direction of time.

    Module 00  ·  Reading rules

    How to read a claim

    Start here, because it is the habit that makes the rest safe. Most physics writing you meet blends what is established with what is hoped for. This project refuses to.

    Every statement in this framework carries a label saying how much it costs to believe. There are three, and once you can spot them you can read anything here without being sold to.

    Forced Assumed Not claimed

    Forced means it follows from the four starting assumptions and nothing else. Very little qualifies — five results in total.

    Assumed means an extra assumption was added, and that assumption is named and numbered. Most of the interesting physics lives here. Because the assumption has a name, you can always ask the useful question: what happens if it is false?

    Not claimed means the project says plainly that it has nothing to offer. These are written down on purpose, in a list, so that nobody can quietly forget them.

    Why this matters

    When you read “gravity is derived here,” you have somewhere to go. You ask which label it carries, and there is an answer. In Module 06 the answer turns out to be: assumed, resting on one specific unproven thing.

    The trap it avoids

    A result that quietly borrows an assumption looks stronger than it is. The whole point of labelling is that the reader, not the author, gets to decide whether the price was worth paying.

    Module 01  ·  The starting point

    Four assumptions, and that is all

    Physics usually begins with space, time, and things moving in them. This begins with none of those.

    The entire ontology is four sentences. Everything else in the course is either squeezed out of these or is an extra assumption with a name.

    IDIn plain language
    O1There is structured information — things made of parts, where two things can genuinely share a part rather than each having a copy.
    O2There is no master clock. Order gets built up locally, one step at a time, by whatever is doing the stepping.
    O3When several things could happen next, what happens is whatever disturbs the existing structure least.
    O4The options that did not happen do not vanish. They are still there; they are just no longer yours.

    Notice what is missing: no space, no time as a background, no particles, no fields, no probability. Those all have to be earned later, and some of them are never earned.

    Notice also that O3 is the only dynamical law. There is no force law here. There is a preference for the least disruptive move, and the rest of this course is working out what “least disruptive” costs.

    Check yourselfO4 says unchosen options stay real. Why would a theory bother saying that, instead of just letting them disappear? (Module 07 gives the payoff.)

    Module 02  ·  The picture

    Knots and strings

    Here is the one image the whole course runs on. Hold on to it and you will follow everything that comes after.

    Picture the world as knots tied together with strings. When two knots hold the same string — not a copy of it, the same one — they are sharing. Sharing is the whole of structure in this theory.

    Now: to pull any part of this away from the rest, you must cut strings. So we count three things whenever anything happens.

    CountWhat it counts
    SStrings you had to cut.
    BLoose ends you left dangling.
    DWhether the thing is still recognisably itself afterwards — one or zero.

    Add those up with weights and you have the cost of a move. Assumption O3 then says the obvious thing: the world takes the cheapest move available.

    That is the entire machine. Everything from here — the area of a black hole, why everything falls at the same rate, why time has a direction — is this counter, applied somewhere new.

    Worth knowing

    “Heavy” in this picture means many strings. A thing with a lot of shared structure is hard to shift, because shifting it disturbs all of them. That is what inertia is here.

    Already an assumption

    That cost is a number at all — rather than just “this is worse than that” — is an extra assumption. It was later traced back to more basic rules about comparing and combining, but it is not free.

    Module 03  ·  The centrepiece

    Only the edge counts

    This is the single most important thing in the course, and you can see it directly. Drag the scene to rotate it.

    To separate a region from everything else, you cut the strings that cross its surface. The strings buried inside are never touched — they are still tied at both ends, both ends inside. So the cost of separating something does not depend on how much is in it.

    Scene 01 — the cut counter

    drag to rotate
    Knots inside—
    Strings inside—
    Crossing the surface—
    Cost to separate—
    Push structure inside all the way up. The interior fills with hundreds of strings — and the cost to separate the region does not move. Now push strings crossing the surface: the cost tracks it exactly, one for one.

    That is a real result, not a simplification for the course. It is forced by the counter itself: the counter only ever counts severed strings, and severing happens at a boundary.

    Physicists have a name for the idea that what is inside a region is governed by what is on its surface: holography. In most approaches it is a surprising discovery that has to be argued for. Here it is not imported at all. It is just the shape of the counter.

    Forced

    That label is the strong one from Module 00: this needs the four assumptions and the counter, and nothing else.

    What it says

    Separation cost is a surface quantity. Grow the contents a hundredfold and nothing changes. This is where every later result about area comes from.

    What it does not say

    Nothing here gives you area in square metres. “Surface” so far means “the set of strings you would cut” — a count, not a measured size. Turning that into geometry is Module 06's unpaid bill.

    Module 04  ·  The honest measurement

    Only differences are real

    Here is an awkward question. If everything is a count of strings — who decided how thick a string is?

    Nobody did. And that is a problem, because you could redraw every string in the picture as ten thinner strings without changing anything physical. Do that, and every count you have made multiplies by ten.

    So which count was the right one? Neither. Absolute counts are an artefact of how finely you chose to draw.

    Scene 02 — the redrawing problem

    drag to rotate
    the statethe baseline
    Strands drawn—
    Total count (absolute)—
    Distance from baseline—
    Two arrangements are shown: the state on the left, the baseline on the right. Refining adds strands to both, identically — so when you ask how far apart they are, the added strands cancel exactly. The absolute number runs away; the difference does not move at all.

    The fix is to stop asking for an absolute amount and only ever ask: how far is this from the baseline? The baseline is the emptiest arrangement — the vacuum. That quantity is called relative entropy, and it is immune to the redrawing.

    This is not a technicality. Physicists have known for decades that when you try to compute the entanglement entropy of a region of space you get infinity, and the infinity depends on an arbitrary choice of how finely to slice things. In this framework that infinity is not a physical fact and not a crisis. It is the redrawing freedom, showing up exactly where you would predict.

    Forced
    The rule to carry forward

    Every quantity in this course that does real work is a difference from the vacuum. Never an absolute. If you catch someone quoting an absolute entropy, they have quoted a drawing convention.

    Not solved by this

    It does not tell you what the vacuum is, only that you must measure against it. Identifying the real one is a separate, open problem.

    Module 05  ·  Bookkeeping

    When do two systems just add up?

    Put two boxes side by side. Is the total disorder the sum of the two? Sometimes exactly. Sometimes not at all — and the gap is worth more than the rule.

    If the two regions share nothing, their counts add. Perfectly, with no correction. Share some strings and they stop adding — and the shortfall is not vague, it is exactly twice the number of shared strings.

    Scene 03 — two regions

    drag to rotate
    A's surface—
    B's surface—
    A and B together—
    Shortfall—
    The amber strings are the ones A and B share; the teal ones run off to the rest of the world. Press draw one boundary around both and the amber strings turn dim — they are now inside the combined region, so the combined boundary cuts neither of them. Each one was counted by A's surface and by B's, and by the combined surface zero times. That is the entire origin of the factor of two.

    That shortfall has a name in physics — mutual information — and it is the standard measure of “these two things are not independent.” Here it is just a counting fact about which boundary cuts what.

    Now the important part, and it is the opposite of what you would guess. You might think a tidy theory would want entropies to always add. It would be a disaster. If they always added, the shortfall would always be zero, which would mean no two regions could ever be correlated — no entanglement, no interference, no measurement, nothing quantum at all. The framework needs this rule to fail, and it fails in exactly one place: wherever things share.

    Forced

    And there is a bonus: this same adding-up rule is what lets cost be a number at all, rather than just a ranking. The bookkeeping of this module and the arithmetic of Module 02 are the same rule, doing two jobs.

    Module 06  ·  Gravity

    One counter doing two jobs

    Drop a hammer and a feather on the Moon and they land together. Everyone knows this. Almost nobody can say why it isn't an outrageous coincidence.

    It is outrageous. Being hard to push and being pulled hard by gravity are two unrelated-sounding properties, and in ordinary physics they are two separate numbers that just happen to be equal, to fifteen decimal places, for every substance ever tested. Newton noticed. Einstein made it an axiom rather than explaining it.

    In this picture there is nothing to explain, because there is only one number.

    PropertyWhat it turns out to count
    How hard a thing is to shoveits strings
    How hard gravity pulls itits strings

    Gravity here is not a force in the usual sense. It is a pull toward arrangements with more ways to be — and how strongly a thing feels that pull depends on how many strings it brings. Which is the same count that makes it sluggish. Divide one by the other and the strings cancel. Everything falls at the same rate.

    Scene 04 — shove it, or drop it

    two objects, one light and one heavy
    Light object3 strings
    Heavy object12 strings
    Result—
    A rope pulls both with the same force, so the heavy one lags — four times the strings, a quarter the acceleration. Switch to gravity and the pull itself scales with the strings, so they stay exactly level. Same objects. The difference is entirely in whether the pull counts strings.

    Run that accounting not for one falling object but for every small region of space and every direction at once, and what comes out the other end is Einstein's field equation — the equation of general relativity. It arrives twice, by two independent routes that were not built to agree, and they agree.

    One more thing falls out. Newton's gravitational constant — the number that says how strong gravity is — stops being a fundamental constant and becomes a description of the vacuum: how densely the empty world shares strings across a surface. Gravity is feeble because the vacuum shares extraordinarily densely.

    Assumed — smooth space
    What was earned

    Everything falling at the same rate stops being a coincidence. And gravity's strength becomes a property of the vacuum rather than a number written on a card.

    What was borrowed

    All of it rests on assuming the step-by-step order of events smooths out into ordinary space with ordinary areas. Nobody has shown that it does. It is the framework's largest single debt, and if it fails this module falls with it. The value of gravity's strength is also not predicted — only re-described.

    Module 07  ·  Time

    Which way is later?

    Films of a pendulum look fine backwards. Films of a smashed cup do not. The difference is smaller and stranger than it looks.

    Two quite different things get called “time,” and keeping them apart is the whole module.

    OrderWhich step comes after which. This comes free — assumption O2 already builds order, one step at a time. It was never in doubt.
    DirectionWhich end of that sequence is the past. This is not settled by order. Run the sequence backwards and every rule about ordering is equally satisfied.

    So where does direction come from? From the one move that cannot be undone.

    Almost everything that happens is a re-tying: strings get rearranged, nothing is lost, and you could put it back exactly. Play it backwards and nothing looks wrong. These moves carry no direction at all.

    But sometimes the world commits — it takes one option and drops the others. By assumption O4 the dropped options are still real. They are simply no longer reachable from where you now are. And there is no move that reaches back.

    Scene 05 — step it forwards, then try to go back

    the Back button is the lesson
    1 of 4 — re-tying
    Move typeRe-tying
    Can it be undone?Yes
    Direction so farNone
    Steps one to three are re-tyings and Back works. Step four cuts, and Back stops working — not because the button is broken, but because there is no move that undoes it. That disabled button is the arrow of time. (Reset demo restarts the illustration — it does not undo the move.)

    Three things in this framework turn out to be the same event, which is the sort of thing you hope for and rarely get: the arrow of time, the production of entropy, and the moment a quantum measurement gets a definite answer. Not three mysteries. One event, viewed through three different ledgers.

    It also explains something you already know but may never have had explained. Why does time obviously flow for a cup, and not obviously at all for an isolated atom? Because committing happens constantly for big things touching an environment, and almost never for small isolated ones. Direction is exactly as common as the world's tendency to look definite — and that is one inequality, not two facts.

    Last consequence, and a pretty one. A clock that only ever re-ties cannot tell you the time: its readings cycle and come back, so it can never distinguish now from four steps ago. To measure duration a clock must do something it cannot take back.

    Forced

    Worth pausing on that label. Unlike Module 06, none of this leans on the assumption that smooth space comes out. The arrow of time sits below gravity in the framework — if the smooth-space assumption failed tomorrow, Module 06 would fall and this module would not.

    What it says

    Order is free; direction is not, and direction is exactly the set of committing moments. Entropy measures the arrow — it does not create it.

    What it does not say

    Nothing here explains why the universe started so orderly. The direction points away from a very tidy beginning, and why the beginning was tidy is untouched — an open problem, listed as one.

    Module 08  ·  The test case

    Two black holes walk into a bar

    In 2015 two black holes merged a billion light years away and the ripple reached Earth. It is the cleanest test this framework has, because the numbers are public.

    A horizon is a boundary you cannot reach back across. Everything from Module 03 applies directly: its size is the count of strings crossing it.

    Now put Module 07 next to Module 05 and the result writes itself. Committing only ever adds to the count, so a horizon can only grow. And early in a merger, when the two holes are still far apart and sharing nothing, their boundaries are separate pieces — so by Module 05 their areas simply add.

    The prediction is checked against four real mergers. Every one grows; none comes close to the ceiling.

    And the reverse is not merely improbable, it is impossible. Split a black hole any way you like and the total area drops. There is no sequence of moves that does it, for the same reason the Back button stopped working in Module 07.

    Honest scoring

    This is not a new prediction — Hawking proved the area theorem in 1971 and these events confirm it. What is new is the route: it arrives here from string-counting and the one-way move, not from the geometry of spacetime.

    Still borrowed

    The step from “string count” to “area in square kilometres” is Module 06's unpaid assumption, again. The inequality is solid; the translation into geometry is not.

    Module 09  ·  The ledger

    What is missing

    A course that ended at Module 08 would have taught you the content and missed the method. This list is the method.

    These are written down in the project itself, numbered, so that nobody has to rediscover them by being disappointed.

    Open problemWhere it bites
    Does smooth space come out?The biggest debt by far. Everything about gravity assumes the step-by-step order of events smooths into ordinary space with ordinary areas. Nobody has shown it does. Modules 06 and 08 are exactly as strong as this and no stronger.
    Why did it start so tidy?Module 07 gives time a direction but not a starting point. Why the early universe was so orderly is untouched. It is a question about initial conditions, not about the rules.
    How strong is gravity?Gravity's strength is re-described as the vacuum's sharing density — not calculated. Same for the cosmological constant: the framework says why the usual calculation is the wrong question, and does not answer the right one.
    A quantum theory of gravityNot here. What Module 06 produces is the equation of state of the underlying structure — closer to deriving the gas laws than to deriving the atom.
    The particlesNo purchase on the particle families, their masses, or the specific forces of the Standard Model. Listed as no purchase, in those words.

    If you take one thing from this course, take the shape of that table rather than any single result in Modules 03 to 08. A framework that keeps its own list of failures is doing something most do not.
