# 04 — Classical Limit

## Expression-theoretic mapping of macroscopic objects

- **Body / ball** — stable, high-share-density expression cluster.
- **Mass** — structural inertia \(m_{\rm struct}\) (disruption cost of changing sequential trajectory).
- **Position / velocity** — parameters of the local sequential projection maintained by an evaluator.
- **Force / bias** — persistent context that systematically raises structural disruption of steps against a preferred direction.
- **Gravity-like ambient bias** — approximately constant \(b_{\rm struct}\) over the relevant region.
- **Trajectory** — the high-\(\kappa\) sequential path selected by repeated lowest-disruption reduction.

## Recovered Newtonian regime

From the structural sequential calculus:

\[
\frac{d^2 x}{dt^2} = -\frac{b_{\rm struct}}{m_{\rm struct}}.
\]

Elementary projectile motion follows by integration. The inverse-acceleration relation for clusters of different share density has been confirmed in the toy simulator.

## Prediction procedure (local evaluator)

1. Maintain a sequential model of the relevant high-share clusters under a consistent strategy.
2. Estimate (or measure) relative structural inertias from share architecture.
3. Identify the ambient bias context and its strength.
4. Evolve the discrete (or continuum) lowest-disruption dynamics until intersection with another cluster or boundary.
5. Because the regime is high-stability / high-\(\kappa\), deviations are suppressed; the projection is reliable within ordinary precision.

## Current reach and gaps

**Closed**
- Single cluster, constant bias → constant acceleration.
- Inverse relation between structural inertia and acceleration under identical bias.
- Parabolic trajectories under uniform bias.

**Still open (required for broader classical physics)**
- Position-dependent bias (conservative forces / potentials).
- Multi-cluster mutual disruption (interaction forces).
- Velocity-dependent residual disruption (drag / dissipation).
- Multi-dimensional sequential parameters (vectors, angular momentum analogues).
- Continuum limit of many weakly coupled excitations (fields).

Each of these is a controlled extension of the same structural cost principle rather than a new ontological primitive.
