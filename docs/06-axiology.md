## Distinction

Primitive functions = elementary capacities (verbs) of reality.  
Input parameters = elementary relata (nouns) that those capacities act upon.

## What can be derived from the metaphysical primitives

From cost, coherence \(\kappa\), conservation (labels), and non-temporal attractors one can extract a thin, formal preference ordering:

- Lower dynamical cost is favored.
- Higher local coherence \(\kappa\) is favored.
- Preservation of conserved labels is required for well-formedness.
- Proximity to high-coherence non-temporal fixed points (\(\mathcal{H}\)) is ranked above proximity to low-coherence fixed points (\(\mathcal{L}\)).

This yields a proto-value theory of the schematic form:

> Prefer transformations and trajectories that minimize cost, maximize \(\kappa\), preserve labels, and increase proximity to high-coherence attractors while avoiding low-coherence attractors.

## What cannot yet be derived

- Intrinsic moral worth of particular contents or agents.
- Categorical obligations or rights independent of cost/\(\kappa\) calculation.
- Thick ethical concepts (justice, dignity, compassion, sacredness) not reducible to the formal measures above.
- The force of “ought” as opposed to the hypothetical “if one wishes to minimize cost / maximize \(\kappa\), then \ldots”.

A full value theory therefore requires at least one additional normative bridge principle. The present system supplies an objective ranking of configurations and transformations but does not yet contain ethics proper.
